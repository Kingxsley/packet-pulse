Why another detector

Signature rules miss what they've never seen

Traditional detection matches known patterns. A DNS flood or DoS burst that doesn't match a signature sails straight through, and a single anomaly-detection model that does catch it usually can't tell you how many normal packets it flagged along the way.

We run four models and show our work

Two unsupervised models (Isolation Forest, Autoencoder) learn what normal traffic looks like with no labels at all. Two supervised models (Random Forest, XGBoost) learn attacks directly. Every one of them is scored on the same held-out packets, and every flag comes with a reason.

How it works

From raw packet to explained verdict

One pipeline, four stages, both capture files. No live database dependency: it runs on the packet data sitting in data/raw/.

01

Ingest

Raw packets load straight from DNSpackets_output.json or Clean_DOS_Capstone.csv: source/dest IP & port, protocol, length, timestamp.

02

Engineer

Four signal features per packet: inter_arrival_time, request_rate, packet_rate (rolling 1s window), packet_length.

03

Train

Isolation Forest & Autoencoder learn "normal" unsupervised. Random Forest & XGBoost learn attacks directly. One shared held-out test split.

04

Score & explain

Every packet gets a verdict, a confidence score, and, for XGBoost, a SHAP breakdown of which feature actually drove the call.
